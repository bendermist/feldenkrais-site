01 · The Method

Learning, not fixing

The Feldenkrais Method® is a way of learning through movement. It was developed by Moshe Feldenkrais (1904–1984), a physicist, engineer and judo teacher, who was interested in how people actually learn to move, and how habits form that quietly limit us.

Instead of stretching harder or training stronger, the work uses slow, attentive and comfortable movement. When effort drops, sensitivity rises: your nervous system can feel differences it normally overrides, and it begins to choose easier options on its own.

Functional Integration®

Individual lessons. Through light, non-invasive touch I guide small movements, so you can feel new possibilities directly rather than by instruction. This is what I offer.

Awareness Through Movement®

The same principles taught in words: verbally guided movement sequences, usually in a group, which you explore on your own.

A short lesson to try

Head and eyes

Sit comfortably, or lie on your back. Do every movement slowly and only as far as is easy.

  1. Turn your head a little to the right and back to the middle, a few times. Let your eyes lead the way. Notice how far you go, and how it feels.
  2. Now keep turning your head to the right, but let your eyes look to the left. Go very slowly. It may feel strange; that's the point.
  3. Rest. Then turn your head and eyes together to the right again. Most people find the turn is easier and goes further.

Nothing was stretched. Your nervous system simply untangled two movements it usually treats as one. That is the heart of the Feldenkrais Method.

Small movements. Big differences.

02 · Functional Integration

A lesson made for one person

What happens in a lesson

You lie, sit or stand, fully clothed, usually on a low, wide table. We begin by talking about what brings you: a movement that has become difficult, discomfort that keeps returning, or a skill you want to refine.

Then, with slow and gentle touch, I move with you: rolling your head, lengthening a leg, clarifying how your ribs follow your spine. Nothing is forced. Lessons last about 45–60 minutes, and no two are the same.

A lesson is

  • a conversation through movement and touch
  • calm, slow and comfortable
  • tailored to you and to that day
  • learning you take into ordinary life

A lesson isn't

  • massage or manipulation
  • a workout or stretching routine
  • a set of exercises to get “right”
  • a substitute for medical care

Who comes to lessons

  • People wanting more ease in everyday movement
  • Recurring neck, back or shoulder discomfort
  • Musicians, dancers and athletes refining skill
  • Recovery after injury, alongside medical care
  • Older adults seeking balance and confidence
  • Desk-bound bodies that feel stuck
  • Anyone simply curious about how they move

05 · Questions

Before your first lesson

What should I wear?

Comfortable, everyday clothes you can move in, such as trousers rather than a skirt. Avoid belts and bulky jewellery. Shoes come off for the table.

Does it hurt?

No. Lessons stay within what is comfortable, and movements are small and slow. You can ask me to stop or change anything at any time.

How many lessons will I need?

Many people feel a clear difference after one lesson. Lasting changes usually come over a series, so we'll talk about what makes sense for you after the first meeting.

How is it different from massage or physiotherapy?

Feldenkrais is education rather than treatment. I don't work on muscles or adjust joints. I help your nervous system learn new options, which you keep using after the lesson.

Can I come if I have a medical condition?

Often, yes. Please tell me about it beforehand. Lessons are educational and don't replace diagnosis or treatment by a doctor; they work well alongside it.
